the brief we have of the project is as follows: QuickBill - Point of Sale System A retail shop needs software to run its daily operations — from managing who has access, to tracking products, processing sales, generating invoices, and understanding business performance. Build it as a working web application. THE SCENARIO Meera owns a stationery and electronics shop. She needs a system she can use at the counter every day — something she can log into securely, manage her products, process sales, and understand how the business is doing. She has no technical background. The system must be fast to use, impossible to accidentally break, and give her a clear picture of her business at any moment. You are the engineer. Every decision about structure, access, workflow, and presentation is yours to make. WHAT YOU NEED TO BUILD Authenticatio 1 Login The system must be behind a login. No part of the application should be accessible without authenticating first. Implement a simple login screen - a user must enter credentials to access the system. THINK CAREFULLY What happens when someone enters wrong credentials? What happens if they try to access a page directly by URL without logging in? How does logout work? These details reveal how a candidate thinks about basic security. 2 Product management Catalogue Meera needs to maintain a catalogue of everything she sells. She should be able to add new products, update existing ones, and remove items she no longer carries. The catalogue is the single source of truth for everything else in the system — if it is wrong, everything downstream is wrong. VALIDATION MATTERS What happens when someone enters an impossible price? A duplicate product name? An empty form? The system should make bad data entry impossible, not just report it after the fact. 3 Stock management Inventory Every product has a quantity on the shelf. Stock must reduce automatically when a sale is made and must never go negative. Meera needs to know at a glance which products are running low before they run out — not after. She must also be able to restock items when new supplies arrive. What counts as "running low" is your decision. Make it and defend it. THE CONSISTENCY PROBLEM A sale and a stock deduction must succeed together or not at all. If a bill is confirmed but stock does not update — or vice versa — Meera's data is permanently wrong. This is the hardest engineering problem in this task. 4 Order management Billing At the counter, staff build a bill by adding products and quantities. The system must show a running total. When a sale is confirmed it must be saved permanently - with everything needed to reconstruct exactly what was sold, to whom, at what price, and when. • A confirmed order cannot be edited - only cancelled • Cancellation must fully reverse the stock impact of that order • Attempting to sell more than what is in stock must be blocked clearly - not silently ignored 5 Invoice generation Billing document Every confirmed order must produce an invoice — a clean, professional document a customer could be handed. The invoice must be printable directly from the browser. Think about what a real invoice looks like and what information it must contain. You decide the layout and content. DESIGN TEST A well-designed printable invoice with no extra engineering is worth more than a poorly laid out one with complex generation logic. Prioritise clarity over complexity here. 6 Dashboard & smart analysis Business intelligence Meera needs to understand her business at a glance when she opens the system each morning. Build a dashboard that surfaces the most important information without her having to ask for it. Go beyond just showing numbers — help her notice things she might otherwise miss. What you choose to show, and how you choose to show it, is entirely your call. There is no fixed list of metrics or charts. WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE Meera opens the dashboard and within 30 seconds knows: how today is going, what needs her attention, and what has been selling. Design for that moment. 7 Reports & export Bonus Build a reports section where Meera can view sales and stock data over any time period che chooses. Reports must be exportable to Excel so she can share them with her accountant. You decide what data the reports contain and how they are structured in the export. EVALUATOR NOTE Excel export tests whether the candidate can produce real business output - not just render data on screen. A clean, well-labelled spreadsheet with the right columns is a strong signal. A broken download or a flat sheet with no structure is not. 8 Al-powered add-on Bonus Integrate any LLM AP| into the system to make it smarter. The integration must solve a real problem for Meera — not be a gimmick. A few examples to spark thinking: • Auto-generate product descriptions when a new item is added to the catalogue • A natural language search — "show me all orders above 500 from last week" • Summarise the day's sales in plain language for the dashboard • Suggest restocking quantities based on recent sales patterns These are examples, not a checklist. You may build something entirely different. The feature must use a real LLM API call - not a mock or hardcoded response. Use any free-tier LLM API available to you. EVALUATOR NOTE This tests whether a candidate can integrate an external Al service into a real workflow meaningfully - prompt design, response parsing, error handling, and UX around async calls. A loading state while the LLM responds, a fallback when it fails, and a well-crafted prompt are all strong signals.
